01 / THE BASICS

First, what
is AI?

AI is software that uses inputs to produce predictions, recommendations, decisions, or new content. [1]

Many systems learn patterns from examples instead of receiving a separate instruction for every situation. A spam filter classifies messages; a generative model creates text or images. Training shapes the model. Using it means applying those learned patterns to a new input.

A chatbot’s fluent answer can feel like understanding. But fluency does not guarantee truth: generated answers can contain invented facts and references. [2]

The part we

Useful does not mean harmless.

01

Confident mistakes

AI may invent a convincing answer. Verify important claims against original sources, especially before acting on them. [2]

02

Bias & privacy

Systems can reproduce harmful biases or expose sensitive information. Ask who might be disadvantaged, and keep private data out of unapproved tools. [2]

03

Seeing is not believing

The FTC warns that scammers can clone a familiar voice. Verify urgent money requests by calling a number you already trust. [3]

The cloud is physical.

The IEA’s 2026 outlook estimates global data-centre electricity use at 485 TWh in 2025 and projects 950 TWh in 2030. These figures cover all data centres, not AI alone. [4]

MY PERSPECTIVE

Progress needs care.

AI could support more accessible services and faster research. Its benefits will depend on who can access it and who remains accountable.

More electricity demand can strain local grids; it does not make blackouts inevitable. Planning, efficiency, and reliable supply matter. [4]

My view: keep human approval for consequential automated actions. Limit permissions, test security, and keep a manual fallback when a service fails. Dependence should never remove our ability to act.

BEHIND THE PAGES

How this site was made.

The content moves from a plain-language explanation of AI to everyday examples, risks, and future choices. The design takes an editorial approach: a white background, oversized serif headings, generous margins, and small green and pink accents. Subtle curved details keep a trace of Art Nouveau without turning the page into a decorative poster.

Real photographs from Unsplash give the page texture. The flowers are a visual metaphor; the workspace and server images connect the story to daily life and physical infrastructure. Photo credits link to their sources. Semantic HTML gives the page its structure, CSS Grid arranges the layouts, and a small JavaScript interaction switches between practical examples. The electricity comparison uses labeled bars and a text alternative.

ChatGPT helped research the sources, draft the text, and write the code. Claims were checked against the linked publications, while forecasts and interpretation are marked separately. Local checks covered file paths, internal links, image files, word count, and interaction logic. Before submitting, I need to review the wording and test the page in a browser on desktop and mobile, including keyboard navigation and the published GitHub Pages version.
